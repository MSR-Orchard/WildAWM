🔭Overview

Computer-use agents increasingly learn by acting in executable environments. Real computers are a poor place to do that: their state is hard to reset, online services drift, and exploratory actions can have real consequences. Synthetic environments restore control, but most pipelines build a single interface at a time, such as the browser, tool calls, or the terminal.

WildAWM synthesizes a whole personal computer for each persona, one day at a time, so websites, MCP services, and local files share one evolving state. It then writes cross-interface (XAI) tasks grounded in these worlds and keeps only those that pass five execution-based quality gates. The entire pipeline is powered by open-source models, without any proprietary models.

  • 341 long-horizon tasks. Some take over 100 agent steps, and GPT-6-Astra reaches only about 60% pass@1.
  • Transfer to unseen benchmarks. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Qwen3.5-4B trained only in WildAWM worlds improve on all eight out-of-distribution benchmarks: web agents (Online-Mind2Web, WebArena), claw agents (QwenClawBench, ClawEval), and computer-use agents (CUA-Gym, OSWorld-Verified, OSWorld 2.0, Agents’ Last Exam). They gain +10.58 and +8.21 points on average.
  • Open-source end to end. Synthesis runs on three open-source models and six agent harnesses, without relying on any proprietary models.

Propose

From a world state, the generator explores the live environment and proposes tasks that need all three interfaces. A diversity gate keeps the proposals least similar to accepted tasks, and each task ships with a verifier, a rubric, and a hint.

Roll out

Heterogeneous agents attempt every task: six rollouts, half with the hint, drawn from 18 model–harness pairs. Success counts become evidence of solvability and difficulty.

Judge

Each rollout receives three verdicts: from the synthesized verifier, from an LLM judge that reads the task, rubric, and trajectory, and from an agent judge that inspects the world before and after.

  • Larger models gain more. The 35B-A3B model improves most on ClawEval (+16.67), OSWorld-Verified (+16.06), WebArena (+13.72), and CUA-Gym (+12.22).
  • The gap to frontier models narrows. The 35B-A3B average rises from 42.84 to 53.42; it matches GLM-5.2 on WebArena (67.26) and exceeds GPT-5.5 on Online-Mind2Web (80.77 vs. 72.22).

💡Findings

  • Hard, yet solvable

    GPT-6-Astra reaches about 60% pass@1 on the validation tasks and still fails 27.3% of them after eight attempts. Yet every task is solved by at least one of 24 model–harness agents.

  • Verifiers agree with judges

    On 100 sampled rollouts, the synthesized verifier matches the majority verdict of agent judges 95% of the time, with Cohen's κ of 0.90.

  • Richer, more diverse worlds

    A WildAWM environment state has a median of 52.4K tokens, against 1.1K for CUA-Gym, and it still scores higher Vendi diversity at every sample size.

  • Synthesis keeps scaling

    Accepted tasks reach 134 domains and 91 task types at a 53% acceptance rate, without clear saturation.

  • SFT teaches switching, RL teaches persistence

    Fine-tuning on GLM-5.2 trajectories raises the interface-switch rate from 18.4% to 24.7%. RL then adds long-horizon solving: between 40 and 120 steps, the share of solved tasks grows by 17.0 points for WildAWM, against 9.7 for SFT alone.

  • Gains stay within the teacher's reach

    Splitting the out-of-distribution tasks into difficulty quartiles, WildAWM gains most on the middle two (+19.9 and +12.2 points). On the hardest quartile, no variant gains clearly.
